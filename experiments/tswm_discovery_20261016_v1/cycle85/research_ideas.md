# Research judgment from the anchored calibration screen

The current development question was whether coupled sensor and dynamics changes require a distinct new repair mechanism. C85 first tests a physically anchored, fully observed Markov subcase using existing adaptations and ordinary simulators. The final casewise decision is in experimental_results.md.

Do not turn a frozen aligner's expected dynamics-shift failure, an inadequate source fit, or an approximate latent model losing to a low-dimensional regression into an innovation claim. A new mechanism would need an advantage over the competent joint/output-space controls, not only over Frozen.

The question's harder variants remain possible research territory, not established gaps. Partial observation or larger dimension must enter through a concrete scientific requirement and an adequate belief/state-inference baseline; making the experiment harder until every model scores poorly is not discovery.

No new method is proposed in C85. No final direction is accepted merely because the screen contains many fits. Further work must start from a reproducible unexplained failure and distinguish it from source training, coverage, model selection, information and capacity limitations. The overall general time-series world-model objective remains unchanged.
