# Do not rename robust modular inference as a new mechanism

[Carmona & Nicholls, Semi-Modular Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06804) develops directed partial control of information flow between misspecified modules, including a criterion for selecting the influence. [Nicholls et al., valid belief updates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09706) studies coherence of the resulting non-additive update. [Frazier & Nott, posterior risk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10911) formalizes bias/variance tradeoffs: completely removing feedback can lose information; a partial cut can dominate a complete cut under its conditions.

Implication for the C83 rescue hypothesis: noticing that a misspecified neighbor model could bias another module, then suppressing its influence, is not new. A pretrained target-local mechanism model would still need to specify which conditional prior/likelihood is changed and why standard cut/semi-modular alternatives are insufficient. Joint trajectory acceptance in synthetic generation concerns a selected prior; that is not automatically identical to likelihood feedback in those papers. This distinction is a question to test, not evidence of novelty.

Current evidence verifies source filtering and shows local field/rollout errors. It does not yet attribute an error to a selected prior, establish an amortized posterior interpretation of FIM's point estimator, or justify a new training scheme. No large pretraining or claim of a solved gap is authorized by these observations alone. Ordinary research work remains authorized; this is a scientific evidence boundary, not a permission gate.
