# Negative evidence and resulting decision

The immediately preceding broad candidate was: identify whether observation or dynamics changed, update the correct component, and thereby preserve simulation. This is not ready to become a paper topic.

1. Generic observation alignment, change-factor adaptation, online encoder/predictor updates and frozen-model residual corrections have direct prior art, recorded in literature_review.md.
2. Without a physical anchoring contract, a component label can depend on the chosen latent coordinates. Two different repairs can agree on every allowed control. Requiring a classifier to choose between such repairs creates an artificial error.
3. The small ordinary linear control succeeds without recovering a cause label. No advantage of a new world-model method follows from this audit.
4. Differences between do(x_j=c) and do(z_j=c) after z=g(x) concern different interventions. They are not evidence that a learned simulator failed an action it was actually given.
5. This does not refute restricted causal-identification theorems, solve nonlinear partial observation, or demonstrate the performance of any of the five cached neural baselines.

Decision: do not launch a new architecture or long training around unanchored component attribution. Retain only the narrower development question about observable action-response calibration under an explicit physical contract. It remains unverified. No final research direction is accepted.
