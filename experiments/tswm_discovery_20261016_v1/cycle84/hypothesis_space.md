# C84 competing hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Test / evidence | Current judgment |
|---|---|---|
| H0: an unknown observation transformation can be absorbed by a changed transition model without changing allowed action outcomes. | Analytic conjugacy construction; 192 trajectory pairs, 12 independently integrated ODE pairs. | Supported for the stated model class. Established mathematics. |
| H1: correctly naming the changed component is always necessary for correct action-conditioned simulation. | The two repairs agree on all tested controls; a linear output-space fit predicts correctly without naming a component. | The universal claim is refuted. This does not establish general neural adaptation success. |
| H2: different interventions on identically named latent coordinates imply failure on the physical action API. | Such operators refer to different physical quantities after a coordinate change. The common action responses still agree. | Rejected as an evaluation argument. |
| H3: modern adaptive world models have a persistent action-response failure after coupled changes, even with adequate physical anchors and competent joint-estimation controls. | No such experiment completed here. | Unproven; not accepted as a research gap. |
| H4: controlled action contrasts help calibrate those responses beyond existing adaptation and active identification. | No new method implemented. Known equivariance, identification and experimental-design methods are close alternatives. | Development hypothesis only; needs novelty and empirical evidence. |

An observation timeout cannot be used to restart C71. A source-code inspection cannot be used as a reproduction. An identifiable special case cannot establish universal identifiability, and an ambiguous unrestricted case cannot invalidate a theorem with additional assumptions.
